# Case Studies Source Archive

Captured on 27 July 2026 from the public Online Marketing Help case-study hub and each linked project page.

This is the permanent editorial source record for the redesigned case-study pages. It excludes repeated site navigation, newsletter forms and footer content, but preserves the meaningful project copy, work described, published result claims and testimonials. It is deliberately separate from the page components so future edits can be checked against the source before publication.

## Archive rules

- All percentages, rankings, durations, client identities and testimonials below are source-page claims, not independently verified facts.
- Public pages must label result figures as source claims until the client supplies the date range, baseline, analytics source and publication approval.
- The source contains duplicated and contradictory content. Those issues are recorded rather than silently corrected.
- “Google My Business” is retained in source notes; current public copy may use “Google Business Profile.”
- Original `/project/` URLs are retained as provenance. Redesigned routes live under `/case-studies/`.

## Cross-source integrity findings

1. `Cars`, `Bar`, `ECommerce` and `Restaurant` reuse the same Keens Steakhouse narrative, address, testimonial and digital-marketing copy. Their titles, feature images and some result counters differ.
2. `Craft Business` tells a CraftyCreations SEO story, but the metadata row names “Keens Steak House,” “4 years,” and a New York address.
3. The `ECommerce` counters were inconsistent between rendered retrievals. One observation returned 9%, 6%, 12% and 7%; a later rendered source returned 17%, 11%, 23% and 13%. The feedback counter rendered as 29%, while its explanatory sentence says 96%.
4. `Cars` and `Restaurant` both publish 78% “More Reservations,” although their hub labels suggest different website sectors.
5. Several pages repeat “After launching the website…” in both strategy and results sections without identifying a launch date or comparison period.
6. The Fine Dining “Added Bonus” sentence ends mid-thought after “driven by their 1st”.
7. Client names appear to be illustrative or anonymised on at least some pages. The Clothing page explicitly says the company name and viewer details were changed.
8. None of the result blocks supplies enough evidence to validate attribution independently. Use the figures only with a visible verification label.

## 1. Bakery

- Source: <https://onlinemarketinghelp.co.uk/project/bakery/>
- Source title: “SEO Case Study Optimising Sweet Success: A Bakery's SEO Journey”
- Client shown: Sweet Delights Bakery
- Duration shown: 4 years
- Category shown: SEO
- Suggested service relationships: Search Engine Optimisation, WordPress Development

### Source overview

Sweet Delights Bakery is described as a family-owned local bakery selling cakes, pastries and bread. The stated objective was to improve online visibility, organic website traffic, online sales and enquiries. The stated challenges were limited online visibility and competition from established local bakeries.

### Work described

- Keyword research around bakery products and local searches.
- Website content, metadata and description optimisation.
- Local SEO, including Google My Business, NAP consistency, local backlinks and location pages.
- Blog content about baking tips and cake decorating, supported by social sharing.
- Page-speed, mobile-responsiveness and technical/on-page improvements.

### Published result claims — verification required

- Six-month implementation period.
- 50% increase in organic search traffic.
- 83% of customer traffic from new users.
- 51% reduction in broken pages.
- 8.3% conversion rate, described as measured in Google Analytics 4.

### Source testimonial

Attributed to Penny Williams, Owner, Sweet Delights Bakery. It praises the team’s professionalism, keyword analysis, strategy and attention to the bakery’s needs. Client approval and the exact wording must be confirmed before publication.

### Editorial note

The source does not state the baseline dates, analytics property, conversion definition, order volume or revenue value behind the percentage claims.

## Future evidence checklist

Before removing verification labels from any redesigned case study, collect:

- Written client approval for the company name, testimonial and publication.
- The service agreement or final scope of work.
- Start and end dates for every comparison.
- Baseline and final values, not percentages alone.
- Analytics property/report name and metric definition.
- Attribution method for SEO, paid media, CRO, sales, reservations or footfall.
- Approved screenshots with sensitive data removed.
- Confirmation that any displayed person is the real authorised client representative.
- Clarification of every source conflict recorded above.
